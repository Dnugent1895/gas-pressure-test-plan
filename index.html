▶ Overview & Status
Important: Confirm the exact pressure, test duration, gauge type, and appliance-disconnect requirements with El Dorado County before starting.
Safety Notice: Natural gas work can be dangerous. Do not pressure-test the gas meter, PG&E side, or connected appliances. The goal is to test the house gas piping only. If you smell gas after returning service, leave the area and call 911 or PG&E.

This checklist covers a whole-house natural gas pressure test for county inspection. It assumes an existing ~30-year-old residence with five gas appliances: indoor fireplace, stove, dryer, HVAC/furnace, and water heater.

Test concept: Shut off gas at the meter. Disconnect house-side piping from the meter. Disconnect and cap appliance branches. Install a pressure test gauge on the house-side piping. Pressurize with air to the county-required test pressure. The county inspector verifies the gauge holds pressure. After inspection, release test air, reconnect the system, leak-check, purge air, and restart appliances safely.

Disclaimer: This is a planning and tracking tool, not a substitute for a licensed plumber, PG&E requirements, or El Dorado County code. If gas odor is present after return to service, leave the area and call PG&E or 911.

▶ Test Objective

The purpose is to pressure-test the house gas piping system, not the appliances, not the flexible appliance connectors, and not the gas meter.

Correct concept:
Meter / utility side: shut off and isolated.
House piping side: disconnected from meter, capped at appliance branches, connected to pressure gauge, and pressurized with air.

▶ Meter Disconnect, Bleed-Off, and Return-to-Service Safety Notes
High-risk work area: Opening the house gas piping at the meter, bleeding natural gas, reconnecting the meter connection, purging air, and relighting appliances are the highest-risk steps in this plan. Use this section as a safety checklist, not as a substitute for a licensed plumber, PG&E guidance, or El Dorado County requirements.

Before disconnecting from the meter

Meter-side disconnect concept

Concept only: The meter/utility side stays shut off and isolated. The test gauge connects to the house-side piping only. Do not pressurize the meter or PG&E side.

Bleeding / depressurizing before opening piping

Do not casually vent gas indoors. Any residual gas must be handled safely. If you are unsure how to safely depressurize the house-side piping, stop and have a plumber handle this step.

After county inspection: releasing test air pressure

Return-to-service safety notes

Important: After the pressure test, the gas piping contains air, not fuel gas. Reconnecting and purging must be done carefully. A plumber is strongly recommended for reconnecting, purging, relighting, and confirming safe operation.
▶ Wednesday Afternoon — After the Inspection Passes
Do not simply remove the gauge and turn gas back on. The system will contain air from the pressure test. Reconnection, leak-checking, purging, and relighting must be done carefully.
▶ Final Checklist
Emergency Reminder: If you smell gas, hear gas escaping, feel unsure about a connection, or cannot safely relight an appliance, stop immediately. Leave the area and call PG&E or 911.
